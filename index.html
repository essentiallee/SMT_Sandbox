SIMULATIONS, MODELS, TWINS / 04

NEA to NYC Zoning:
A Speculative Simulation for Cultural Funding from 2022 to 2025

How much funding continuity can be preserved while more New York City neighborhoods meet an annual cultural funding threshold?

5 boroughs · 4 fiscal years
01 / THE RECORD · TWIN

Direct NEA funding transactions for named NYC organizational recipients under assistance listing 45.024. The FY2022 starting record is observed; later allocations in the simulation are hypothetical.

The map groups recipient addresses into 2020 Neighborhood Tabulation Areas (NTAs). Funding marks the location of an organization, not the reach of its programs.

02 / THE RULE · MODEL
2022Begin with observed signed net obligations. No minimum grant is imposed on the historical record.
2023Preserve a share of this year’s actual budget according to the previous simulated funding shares. Test redistribution toward neighborhoods below the threshold.
2024Carry the selected allocation forward. Organizations become eligible from their first recorded award transaction.
2025Compare complete trajectories for continuity and neighborhood-years meeting the fixed threshold.

70% protected funding, a $25,000 neighborhood threshold and a $10,000 minimum positive organizational allocation are simulation assumptions, not NEA policies.

03 / THE RUN · SIMULATION

Move the clock, then change the assumptions. Select a point on the comparison chart to inspect a different four-year trajectory. Purple points are non-dominated among the tested alternatives; pink marks your selection.

How the tested allocations work

Each year first reserves the protected share in proportion to the prior simulated allocations. Protected amounts below $10,000 are topped up from available money, largest first; where that is unaffordable, they become zero. This repair may change the effective protected share, especially at 100%.

The test tries redistribution effort levels of 0%, 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% independently in FY2023, 2024 and 2025: 125 trajectories. These are internal search choices, not additional user assumptions. The balance follows prior shares. Neighborhood targeting visits the least expensive threshold gaps first, adding funds to one eligible organization per neighborhood. Existing recipients with the largest allocation are preferred; ties use recipient ID.

Unused redistribution money returns to prior shares. Positive allocations below the minimum are removed and their money redistributed among funded recipients. Every simulated year spends exactly its observed eligible budget, to the cent. Newly eligible organizations can receive neighborhood-targeted funding.

Continuity is 100 × (1 − mean annual half-sum of absolute changes in organizational funding shares), over the three simulated transitions. Higher is more continuous. Coverage counts NTA-years meeting the selected threshold across FY2022–2025; the FY2022 count is constant across alternatives.

The initial selection gives equal weight to normalized continuity and coverage, with ties favoring coverage. This is a display choice, not an observed preference. The chart is the Pareto frontier of this tested set, not proof of a global optimum. A trajectory is dominated if another is at least as good on both measures and strictly better on one.

04 / WHAT THIS CANNOT SEE

No causal connection between funding and zoning is assumed. Pink amendment areas appear by actual effective date; the current zoning district snapshot is not used as a historic baseline.

Negative reversals stay in the observed data and net budgets. Only positive FY2022 amounts define starting simulation shares. Observed amounts are never overwritten by simulated grants.

17 organizations could not be assigned to an NTA. They remain in budgets and continuity calculations, but are excluded from neighborhood coverage and targeting. Their totals are shown separately. Earliest recorded addresses are held fixed; later moves and service areas are not modeled.

45.025 partnership funding, subawards and unnamed recipients are excluded. Named direct recipients may include regranting intermediaries; that role has not been independently audited. Eligibility before FY2022 cannot be established from this extract.

NTAs include parks and other special areas. Kingsbridge–Marble Hill spans Bronx and Manhattan tracts and remains one NTA. Geocodes are address-range estimates, not surveyed building locations. Map boundaries are simplified only for display.
